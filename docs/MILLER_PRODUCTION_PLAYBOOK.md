# MILLER Production Playbook

## Purpose

This document is the primary production standard for all MILLER visual cases,
campaign assets, stills, motion tests, and final video work.

The goal is to protect creative consistency, control generation costs, and
keep every decision reviewable before moving to a more expensive stage.

## Core production route

Follow this sequence unless the user explicitly approves a different route:

1. Commercial brief
2. Look development
3. Master still approval
4. Supporting keyframes
5. Keyframe approval
6. Cheap motion test in Remotion or HyperFrames
7. Motion test review
8. `NEEDS RUNWAY` decision
9. Explicit user confirmation for Runway
10. Final generation, edit, quality control, and delivery

Do not skip forward because a later tool appears faster or more capable.

## 1. Commercial brief before look development

Every production task starts with a concise commercial brief. Establish:

- the business objective;
- the audience;
- the message or product value;
- the intended placement and format;
- the desired emotional response;
- the visual territory and references;
- required subjects, products, locations, or actions;
- exclusions and brand risks;
- duration, aspect ratio, and delivery constraints when relevant.

Resolve material ambiguity before generating look-development images. Do not
use image generation as a substitute for defining the commercial objective.

## 2. Look development before motion

Use image generation to establish the visual world before any video work.
Look development should test the decisions that matter most: location,
architecture or set, casting, wardrobe, light, materials, palette, lens,
composition, atmosphere, and degree of realism.

Produce focused options rather than large undirected batches.

### Look-development limit

Make no more than two normal look-development passes for one direction:

- Pass 1 establishes the direction.
- Pass 2 applies focused corrections from review.

If the direction is still unresolved after two proper passes, stop routine
iteration and propose an Astra escalation for deeper creative reasoning. Do
not start that escalation without the user's approval.

## 3. Master still before video

Select and approve one master still before generating video.

The master still locks the main visual DNA:

- subject and environment;
- art direction;
- composition and scale;
- lighting and time of day;
- materials and color;
- human presence and styling;
- camera language;
- realism and finish.

Do not start video generation while the master still is unapproved.

## 4. Keyframes before motion

After the master still is approved, create the supporting keyframes needed for
the planned sequence. They must feel like one production: the same world,
location, subjects, materials, light logic, and visual language.

Review and approve the keyframes before motion work begins. A storyboard or
shot list may accompany them, but it does not replace visual keyframes.

## 5. Cheap motion test first

Test timing, framing, transitions, camera movement, text placement, and overall
rhythm with Remotion or HyperFrames before using a paid video generator.

The motion test should answer whether the sequence works. Use stills, crops,
parallax, masks, transforms, light effects, and editorial timing as needed.
Keep it inexpensive and easy to revise.

Do not present a motion test as final generated footage.

## 6. Runway gate

Use Runway only when the reviewed motion test demonstrates a requirement that
cannot be achieved to the required quality with the approved stills and the
cheap motion workflow.

Before opening or running Runway, state exactly:

`NEEDS RUNWAY`

Then explain briefly:

- which shot needs it;
- what motion or realism is missing;
- why Remotion or HyperFrames is insufficient;
- the smallest generation scope required.

Runway may be used only after explicit user confirmation given after this
assessment. Earlier general permission does not replace this shot-specific
confirmation.

## 7. Other paid video generators

Do not use Kling, Higgsfield, or any other paid video generator without the
user's explicit permission for that tool and production step.

Do not switch paid providers as a workaround when Runway has not been approved
or when a generation fails.

## 8. Approved route is fixed

Once the user approves a production route, Codex must follow it. Codex may
recommend a change when new evidence creates a clear quality, cost, or
technical problem, but must not apply the change independently.

Before changing the route:

1. describe the issue;
2. propose the smallest useful change;
3. explain its effect on quality, cost, and schedule;
4. receive explicit user approval.

Silence, elapsed time, or approval of a different stage does not authorize a
route change.

## 9. Stage discipline

At the end of each stage:

- show the actual result;
- identify the recommended option;
- state what is approved and what remains open;
- stop before the next gated or paid stage when confirmation is required.

Do not modify the website, publish assets, generate motion, or start paid tools
unless those actions are part of the current approved task.

## Quick gate checklist

- [ ] Commercial brief is defined.
- [ ] Look development is complete within two normal passes.
- [ ] Master still is explicitly approved.
- [ ] Supporting keyframes are consistent and approved.
- [ ] Motion is tested in Remotion or HyperFrames.
- [ ] `NEEDS RUNWAY` is documented if paid generation is necessary.
- [ ] Runway has shot-specific explicit user confirmation.
- [ ] Any other paid video generator has explicit permission.
- [ ] The approved production route has not been changed independently.
